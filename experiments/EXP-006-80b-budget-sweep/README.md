## Research question

Can residency and bytes per token predict the speed of a model never measured before?

## Hypothesis

[HYPOTHESIS] Throughput degradation is primarily explained by resident expert percentage (plus bytes per token). Prediction written before the run: 4.0-5.0 tok/s at a 4 GiB budget (11.7% residency), from the 30B at 12% residency (4.35 tok/s).

## Setup

Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct Q3_K_M (512 experts, 10 active, 34.31 GiB of experts) + store, 4 threads, 4 GiB benchmark (3 reps), then a 4 / 3 / 2 / 1.5 GiB sweep (async reads, 3 reps each).

## Commands

```bat
argine benchmark Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct-Q3_K_M.gguf --expert-store --expert-budget 4G
argine benchmark Qwen3-Next-80B-A3B-Instruct-Q3_K_M.gguf --expert-store --sweep-expert-budget 4G,3G,2G,1.5G
```

## Raw data

`raw/records.jsonl` — the exact records of Argine's `runs.jsonl` used here (sanitised: host and user name only). Full log: `data/raw/argine-v1/runs.jsonl`. `results.csv` is regenerated by `python tools/build_historical_data.py`.

## Result

[MEASURED] 6.96 / 6.87 / 6.81 tok/s at 4 GiB (binary 3d91ef83); sweep (binary 3cac5531): 7.10 / 6.32 / 5.68 / 4.87 tok/s. RAM peak 6.11 GiB at 4 GiB; peak - budget = 2.110 GiB at every budget.

[MEASURED] A further 4 GiB benchmark eight minutes after the sweep, same binary: 5.95 / 5.74 / 5.54 tok/s (-19% vs the sweep's 4 GiB point). Not reported in Argine's README; see EXP-018.

[MEASURED] (Argine README, store counters) 91.2% runtime hit rate, 1,771,302 hits / 170,088 misses.

[DERIVED] Exponent re-derived from raw medians: 0.384 (Argine: 0.384 +- 0.043), vs 0.50-0.58 on the 30B.

## Interpretation

[FALSIFIED] Residency alone does not predict speed: the model with 2.1x the experts was 1.58x FASTER than the 30B at the same residency. Argine attributed this to router concentration (an 11.7% cache serving 91.2% of requests). [DERIVED, Argine2] The 91.2% counts slice re-pins of the up/down nodes; the expert-level hit rate is about 1 - 3 x 0.088 = 74% (EXP-020) — still far above 11.7%, so the concentration explanation survives, with a smaller number.

[FALSIFIED] **Audit closure (2026-10-11).** Resident expert percentage alone is insufficient to predict throughput across the tested MoE models. The pre-run prediction (4.0–5.0 tok/s) was contradicted (6.87 tok/s; 7.10 in the sweep).
- The later 4 GiB run in the same session (5.74 tok/s, −19%) is kept as a documented session anomaly (EXP-018). It still lies above the predicted range and does not erase the falsification.
- [Update, EXP-007] The 80B router is now measured directly and is generally more concentrated than the 30B's. EXP-007 also shows that routing concentration is not a model-agnostic predictor of inter-layer allocation opportunity.
- EXP-006 therefore remains a valid falsification of a residency-only predictor, while the explanation of the cross-model speed difference is only partially resolved (RQ-009, EXP-026).
- The derived ≈74% expert-level hit rate above is the historical derivation. EXP-007 measured 61.4–78.1% at 4 GiB across workloads, on a different binary and prompts.
- No rerun is required for closure ([historical audit](../../docs/research/HISTORICAL-AUDIT-EXP001-006.md)).

## Limitations

[LIMITATION] No router trace of the 80B exists yet, so concentration was inferred from the hit counter, not measured. [SUPERSEDED, 2026-10-11] The 80B router has since been traced directly in [EXP-007](../EXP-007-router-characterization/README.md): it is more concentrated than the 30B (global EWS90 fraction 0.225–0.472 vs 0.327–0.554). This experiment's own measurements are unchanged. The I/O bucket decomposition (44% read, 31% decommit, 17% lock) is from the README's single run.
