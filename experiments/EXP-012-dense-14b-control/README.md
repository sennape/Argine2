## Research question

Within the same 16 GB deployment envelope, does a larger heavily compressed MoE show a quality advantage over a smaller dense model of the same family?

## Hypothesis

[HYPOTHESIS] (the project's founding assumption, as PicoStar16) A larger compressed model 'knows more' than a smaller intact one.

*Audit reading (2026-10-11):* for historical closure, this broad claim is operationalized only as the prediction that the larger compressed 30B would show a clear advantage on the matched-text perplexity proxy used by the experiment.

## Setup

Qwen3-14B dense Q4_K_M (8.38 GiB) vs Qwen3-30B-A3B with IQ2_S experts (9.65 GiB); same tokenizer; perplexity with identical signature (ctx 512, 60 chunks, same text); generation speed, 4 threads.

*Audited setup (2026-10-11), from `raw/records.jsonl`:*
- **Models.** Qwen3-14B dense Q4_K_M, model file approximately 8.38 GiB, and Qwen3-30B-A3B IQ2_S, model file approximately 9.65 GiB. Same tokenizer and model family. Similar footprint within the same 16 GB deployment envelope, not equal footprints. Provenance: the 30B footprint of approximately 9.65 GiB is the Argine v1 README value; the 30B benchmark record's `model.file_size` is 10,289,713,824 bytes (9.58 GiB). The 14B record gives 9,001,753,376 bytes (8.38 GiB).
- **Perplexity.** ctx=512, 60 chunks, reference text hash `da315163291ee5c9`; signature `ctx=512;chunk=60;testo=da315163291ee5c9` for both models.
- **Generation.** CPU backend, 4 threads, 128 generated tokens per repetition, 3 repetitions inside each `argine benchmark` invocation.
- **Binaries.** The 30B throughput record used `llama-noomp@757a17a4`; both 14B throughput records used `llama-noomp@ecd06508`.
- **Sessions.** The measurements were taken in different sessions and on different days: 30B on 2026-10-01 20:22; 14B on 2026-10-02 21:20 and 21:23.
- The throughput comparison is therefore not a same-binary, interleaved architecture experiment.

## Result

[MEASURED] Matched-text perplexity was 13.2766 ± 0.3176 for the dense 14B and 13.6283 ± 0.3290 for the 30B IQ2_S. Historical generation throughput was 3.81–3.83 tok/s for the 14B and 12.97 tok/s for the 30B.

(Original wording, unchanged values: perplexity 13.2766 +- 0.3176 (14B) vs 13.6283 +- 0.3290 (30B IQ2_S); speed 3.81-3.83 vs 12.97 tok/s.)

## Interpretation

[HISTORICAL INTERPRETATION, narrowed by the 2026-10-11 audit] The original experiment described the quality as 'indistinguishable' (perplexity difference 0.35 against 2 sigma 0.91) and the sparse model as 3.4x faster 'at equal quality'. Those statements are broader than the evidence supports. They are superseded by the audit closure below.

[FALSIFIED, HISTORICAL] **Audit closure (2026-10-11).**
- On the matched-text perplexity proxy, the larger compressed 30B did not show the predicted advantage: 13.6283 ± 0.3290 versus 13.2766 ± 0.3176 for the dense 14B.
- EXP-012 therefore remains FALSIFIED for the proxy-scoped historical hypothesis that the larger compressed model would demonstrate a clear perplexity advantage in this deployment regime.
- This result does not establish equal assistant quality, equal knowledge, equal reasoning capability or model equivalence. Cross-model perplexity on one corpus is only a token-prediction proxy. It measures token-prediction behaviour on that corpus and does not establish instruction-following equivalence, tool-use equivalence or general model equivalence.
- The historical generation result, 12.97 versus 3.83 tok/s, is an observed throughput contrast of approximately 3.39x under the recorded configurations. It is not a causal sparse-vs-dense speedup, because the measurements used different runtime binaries (`llama-noomp@757a17a4` vs `llama-noomp@ecd06508`) and different sessions. EXP-011 establishes binary identity as an experimental variable.
- The 14B repeat changed from 3.81 to 3.83 tok/s despite a large reduction in observed system disk reads (17.79 GB → 1.48 GB over the invocation). That supports only the narrow conclusion that its low generation throughput was not materially changed by the first run's paging pressure. It does not remove the cross-binary and cross-session confound against the 30B.
- No rerun is required for historical closure ([historical audit](../../docs/research/HISTORICAL-AUDIT-EXP011-018.md)).

## Limitations

[LIMITATION] Perplexity across different models is a weak proxy for assistant quality.

[LIMITATION, historical audit 2026-10-11]
1. Cross-model perplexity is a weak proxy for assistant quality and knowledge.
2. Only one reference text and signature was used: `ctx=512;chunk=60;testo=da315163291ee5c9`.
3. No equivalence or non-inferiority margin was pre-specified. Overlapping uncertainty, or the lack of a detected advantage, does not establish equivalence.
4. Model footprints are similar but not equal: approximately 8.38 GiB vs 9.65 GiB (9.58 GiB per the 30B benchmark record).
5. Throughput uses different runtime binaries: `llama-noomp@757a17a4` (30B) vs `llama-noomp@ecd06508` (14B).
6. Throughput measurements were taken in different sessions and on different days, and were not interleaved or rotated.
7. The three generation repetitions are within benchmark invocations, not independent rotated launches.
8. EXP-012 predates the Argine2 reproducibility protocol.
9. The observed approximately 3.39x throughput ratio must not be interpreted as a causal architecture coefficient.
